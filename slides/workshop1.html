SECTION 01

What AI Is

What these tools do, which ones to use, and the one data rule.

THE MENTAL MODEL

AI Drafts, You Decide

ChatGPT and Copilot write text by predicting what words should come next, based on patterns in a huge amount of writing.

  • Good at drafting, summarizing, rewriting, and explaining.
  • Can sound confident and still be wrong. They are not search engines or databases.
  • Think of a fast, eager assistant who has read a lot but is sometimes wrong. You check the draft before you use it.

A FEW WORDS YOU'LL HEAR

Four Terms

Prompt
  • What you type into the box: a question, a request, or instructions.
Chat
  • One conversation. It remembers earlier messages in the same chat, not other chats.
Model
  • The AI system underneath the tool. You can use the default.
Hallucination
  • When the AI states something false as if it were true.

TOOLS & DATA

Which Tool, and the One Data Rule

Which tool to use
  • ChatGPT Edu, signed in with your Drexel account
  • Copilot Chat, signed in with your Drexel account
  • Personal ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini: public or low-risk information only
Never paste in
  • Health information about a person
  • Student records or Social Security numbers
  • Identifiable research data
  • When in doubt, leave it out or make the details up

SECTION 02

How AI Gets Things Wrong

And who is responsible when it does.

THREE THINGS TO REMEMBER

Wrong, Biased, and Yours

It Makes Things Up

It predicts likely-sounding text, so it can invent facts, numbers, and citations. Check anything that matters.

It Can Be Biased

It learned from writing that reflects society's inequities, so answers can repeat stereotypes. Our work affects real communities.

You Are Responsible

If your name is on it, you own it: accuracy, fairness, and tone. “The AI wrote it” is never the answer.

SECTION 03

Hands-On

A guided tour, three first requests, and a simple recipe for better answers.

GUIDED TOUR · ~10 MIN · FOLLOW ALONG

ChatGPT Edu, Step by Step

  1. 1Sign in with your Drexel account. Check you are in the Drexel workspace.
  2. 2Find the message box at the bottom of the screen.
  3. 3Type: “In two sentences, what can you help a public health staff member with?”
  4. 4Follow up in the same chat: “Give me three examples.”
  5. 5Copy the answer with the copy icon.
  6. 6Click “New chat.” It does not remember the old one.
  7. 7Find past chats in the list on the left.

ACTIVITY 1 · ~15 MIN

Your First Three Requests

Copy each prompt from the workshop page into ChatGPT Edu, one at a time. There are no wrong answers.

  • Summarize: a short paragraph on pedestrian deaths, in 3 bullet points.
  • Draft: a friendly reminder email to a committee, under 100 words.
  • Explain: what a “data use agreement” is, in plain language.
  • Then follow up: “Make it shorter,” “More formal,” or “Add a sentence about ___.”

ACTIVITY 2 · ~15 MIN

Four Questions Before You Type

PTCF
  • Persona: who should it act as?
  • Task: what do you want it to do?
  • Context: what does it need to know?
  • Format: what should the answer look like?
Try both
  • Short: “Write an email about a meeting.”
  • Full: “You are an experienced department administrator. Write an email announcing a staff meeting Tuesday at 10 in Room 204 about the new travel policy. Under 100 words, warm tone, date and time first.”

WRAP-UP

What to Take Away

AI writes drafts. You check them and decide.

  • For Drexel work, use ChatGPT Edu or Copilot and follow the data rule.
  • Before you type, ask the four questions: Persona, Task, Context, Format.
  • Before Core 2: do one weekly task (an email, a summary, notes) with ChatGPT Edu, and bring it.
