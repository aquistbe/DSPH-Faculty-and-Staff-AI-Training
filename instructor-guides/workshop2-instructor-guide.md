# Instructor Guide: Core 2

Workshop: Workflow Enhancement  
Length: 60 minutes  
Presentation target: about 15 minutes of slides; the rest is hands-on  
Main tools: Copilot Chat (with enterprise data protection) and ChatGPT Edu  
Participant assumption: they attended Core 1. They can sign in to ChatGPT Edu, type a request, and ask a follow-up. Most are still beginners.

Drexel does not have the Microsoft 365 Copilot license, so there is no Copilot button inside Word, Excel, or PowerPoint. Everything in this session works by pasting text or uploading a file to Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu, then copying the answer back. In Outlook, everyone has the **Summarize** button and the Copilot pane; "Draft with Copilot" and "Coaching" are not available.

## Goals

Participants should leave able to:

1. Pick a weekly task where AI can save time.
2. Get a document, email, or spreadsheet into Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu by pasting or uploading.
3. Use three everyday moves: summarize, rewrite for an audience, make a table.
4. Check the answer before using it (Verified? Disclosed? Yours?).
5. Save one prompt as a reusable recipe.

## Slide Notes

### Slide 3: Welcome Back

Ask for a show of hands: who tried a task after Core 1? Take one or two examples. If few did, say so lightly and move on: "Today you'll leave with two tasks set up."

### Slides 5-6: Where AI Fits

Slide 5: "You frame it, AI drafts the middle, you finish it. The middle is where the weekly hours go."

Slide 6: point at the gold quadrant. "Frequent and low-stakes is where to start." Ask the room to name one task that fits there.

### Slide 7: Two Tools, Same Steps

Say: "Copilot Chat and ChatGPT Edu both take pasted text or an uploaded file. Use whichever you like."

If someone asks about Copilot inside Word or Excel: "Drexel doesn't have that license. You don't need it. Upload the file, ask, and copy the answer back." If they ask about Outlook: the **Summarize** button at the top of a thread works for everyone.

### Slide 8: Same Data Rule

Same rule as Core 1. Drexel lists Copilot Chat (signed in) as approved for High Risk Data, but for this session keep to the simple rule: no health information about a person, no student records, no identifiable research data.

### Slides 10-11: Before You Hit Send

Trace one draft through the three diamonds. Verified: names, dates, numbers. Disclosed: official, research, or external work. Yours: does it say what you mean? Any "no" sends it back. Core 4 covers this in depth.

### Slide 13: Guided Start

Do each step on the projector and pause. Ask for a thumbs-up after the upload step; that's where people get stuck. Common problems:

- No shield icon: they are signed in with a personal Microsoft account. Have them sign out and sign in with their Drexel account.
- Upload won't work: open the file and paste the text.

### Slides 14-17: Activity 1

Ask people to pick a job within one minute. Staff often pick B, faculty A, analysts C. Leave the slide for the most popular job on screen.

Built-in teaching moments:

- **Job A:** read one sentence of the plain-language rewrite aloud and check a fact against the original. The "Next Steps" are made up by the AI; that's fine for a draft and is exactly why you edit.
- **Job B:** the accreditation crosswalk was flagged and put on the September agenda, with no owner. Ask: "Is that a decision, an action item, or an open question?" Summaries often file it wrong.
- **Job C:** 8 of 30 rows have cost per attendee over $12 (Oct EOH and HMP; Dec EOH and HMP; Jan HMP; all three in June). Nov HMP is exactly $12.00 ($300 ÷ 25). A formula using `>` won't flag it; `>=` will. Good moment to show why you check the formula, not just the answer. Attendance dips in Dec-Jan and drops in June.

### Slide 18: Activity 2

Push 2-3 rounds of follow-ups. Useful ones: "Make it shorter." "Put it in a table." "What did you leave out?" "Flag anything I should double-check."

Make sure everyone saves at least one recipe before leaving: a Word document or OneNote page called "My prompts," with the details replaced by [brackets]. Core 3 builds on these.

For the abstracts fallback: the confidence intervals are the numbers most likely to be wrong in the table. Check them live.

## Common Questions

**Why not Copilot inside Word?**  
Drexel doesn't have the Microsoft 365 Copilot license. Uploading to Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu does the same jobs with one extra step: copying the answer back.

**Which tool is better?**  
For these tasks, either. ChatGPT Edu is the one we'll use for saved assistants in Core 3.

**Can I upload real work documents?**  
If they contain nothing on the "never paste in" list, yes. When in doubt, make up the details.

**Should I save the AI's answers?**  
Save the prompts and your edited final products. Don't keep unreviewed AI output as a record of fact.
