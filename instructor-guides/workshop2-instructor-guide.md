# Instructor Guide: Core 2

Workshop: Workflow Enhancement  
Length: 60 minutes, on Zoom  
Presentation target: about 15 minutes of slides; the rest is hands-on  
Main tools: Copilot Chat (with enterprise data protection) and ChatGPT Edu  
Participant assumption: they attended Core 1. They can sign in to ChatGPT Edu, type a request, and ask a follow-up. Most are still beginners.

**Two framing points for the whole session:**

1. **Plan first.** People write a few bullets of their own (the point, the audience, the key facts) before asking AI for anything. AI drafts from that plan or refines what they wrote; it does not replace the plan.
2. **Draft or refine.** An AI draft is fine for routine, low-stakes tasks where the person knows the content. When their expertise, judgment, or voice is the point, or the stakes are high, they write it and AI acts as an editor: tightening language, catching inconsistencies, and finding what's missing, as a list, not a rewrite.

Drexel does not have the Microsoft 365 Copilot license, so there is no Copilot button inside Word, Excel, or PowerPoint. Everything works by pasting text or uploading a file to Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu, then copying the answer back. In Outlook, everyone has the **Summarize** button and the Copilot pane; "Draft with Copilot" and "Coaching" are not available.

## Goals

Participants should leave able to:

1. Pick a weekly task where AI can save time.
2. Start from their own plan, then decide whether AI should draft or refine.
3. Get a document, email, or spreadsheet into Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu.
4. Use AI as an editor: refine language, catch inconsistencies, find what's missing.
5. Check the answer before using it (Verified? Disclosed? Yours?) and save one prompt as a recipe.

## Slide Notes

### Slide 3: Welcome Back

Ask everyone to type one line in the chat: what they tried after Core 1, and whether it helped. Read two or three aloud. If few did it, say so lightly: "Today you'll leave with two tasks set up." Then go over the right-hand box: Zoom and browser side by side, 👍 when done, questions in the chat.

### Slide 5: You Plan It, AI Helps, You Finish It

"Before AI, you write a few bullets: the point, who it's for, the key facts. Two minutes. AI then drafts from your plan or refines what you wrote. And you can check the result against your plan, which is much easier than checking it against nothing."

### Slide 6: When Is an AI Draft OK?

The center of the session. Walk the three columns:

- **AI drafts from your outline:** routine, low stakes, you know the content. A meeting recap or reminder email is fine to draft.
- **You write, AI refines:** when your expertise, judgment, or voice is what people are relying on. A recommendation letter or grant aims that AI wrote are not really yours, even if they read well. Write it, then use AI to improve it.
- **Leave AI out:** sensitive information, and the decision itself.

Ask in the chat: "Name one task of yours for each column." Read a few aloud.

### Slide 7: Three Ways to Refine

"These three prompts are where AI helps most with writing that matters. Notice each one asks for a **list**, not a rewrite. You stay the author; the AI is the second reader who catches what you missed." Inconsistencies are the sleeper hit: dates that don't match, a name spelled two ways, a number that changes between sections.

### Slides 8-9: Tools and Data

Brief. Both tools take pasted text or an uploaded file. If someone asks about Copilot inside Word or Excel: Drexel doesn't have that license, and they don't need it. For data: same rule as Core 1. Drexel lists Copilot Chat (signed in) as approved for High Risk Data, but for this session keep to the simple rule.

### Slides 11-12: Before You Hit Send

Trace one draft through the three diamonds. Verified: check it against your plan and the original. Disclosed: official, research, or external work. Yours: does it say what you mean? Any "no" sends it back. Core 4 covers this in depth.

### Slide 14: Guided Start

Do each step on your shared screen and pause. Ask for a 👍 reaction after the upload step; that's where people get stuck. Point out why summarizing is a safe first job: the content already exists, so every bullet can be checked against it. Common problems:

- **No shield icon:** they're signed in with a personal Microsoft account. Sign out, then sign in with the Drexel account.
- **Upload won't work:** open the file and paste the text.
- **Can't find the file:** repost the link in the chat.

### Slides 15-18: Activity 1

Ask people to pick a job and type A, B, or C in the chat. Leave the slide for the most common choice on screen; switch if the chat asks. Staff often pick B, faculty A, analysts C.

Built-in teaching moments:

- **Job A (find what's missing):** the study description has aims, methods, products, and partners, but no timeline, budget, sample size, team roles, or plan for privacy in street imagery. Ask people to post one gap the AI found in the chat, and whether it's real. This is AI as an editor: it lists gaps; the author decides what to add.
- **Job B (reply from your outline):** the person supplies the two points; AI only turns them into sentences. Check whether the reply added anything they didn't say. Also: the accreditation crosswalk was flagged and put on the September agenda, with no owner. Ask: "Decision, action item, or open question?" Summaries often file it wrong.
- **Job C:** 17 of 60 rows have cost per attendee over $12: Oct EOH, HMP, and Autism Institute; every unit in Dec except Epi & Biostat; Jan HMP, CHP, and Autism Institute; all six in June. Nov HMP is exactly $12.00 ($300 ÷ 25). A formula using `>` won't flag it; `>=` will. A good moment to show why you check the formula, not just the answer. For "what stands out?": attendance dips in December and January and is lowest in June in every unit; Epi & Biostat and UHC draw the most people.

### Slide 19: Activity 2

Hold people to step 1: **a plan before AI.** Ask them to type "plan done" in the chat before opening AI. Then each person decides: routine task → recipe 1 (draft from outline); expertise or high stakes → write it, then recipe 2 (refine). Make sure everyone saves at least one recipe in a "My prompts" document before leaving; Core 3 builds on these.

For the abstracts fallback: the confidence intervals are the numbers most likely to be wrong in the table.

### Slide 20: Wrap-Up

Ask for one line in the chat: one thing they'll try this week. Paste the feedback form link (bottom of the Core 2 page) in the chat.

## Common Questions

**Why write a plan if AI can just draft it?**  
Without your plan, AI fills the gaps with generic or made-up content, and you have nothing to check it against. Two minutes of bullets saves the time you'd spend fixing a draft that went the wrong way.

**When is it fine to let AI write the whole thing?**  
Routine, low-stakes tasks where you know the content: reminders, recaps, announcements. Still from your outline, and still checked.

**Why not Copilot inside Word?**  
Drexel doesn't have the Microsoft 365 Copilot license. Uploading to Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu does the same jobs with one extra step: copying the answer back.

**Which tool is better?**  
For these tasks, either. ChatGPT Edu is the one we'll use for saved assistants in Core 3.

**Can I upload real work documents?**  
If they contain nothing on the "never paste in" list, yes. When in doubt, make up the details.
