AI Workshops for DSPH

Dornsife School of Public Health | Drexel University

A hands-on workshop series for faculty, staff, and graduate students

Welcome

This is a hands-on series introducing AI tools for everyone at the Dornsife School of Public Health—faculty, administrative and research staff, and graduate students. Each session runs one hour: a brief overview followed by activities you can complete in the session.

For 2026–27 the series is organized into four core sessions plus a set of electives and a monthly AI Clinic. The four core sessions are the shared foundation: they run weekly in the fall term and repeat in spring, so you can catch them whenever they fit your year. Electives run once a year, by demand. The Clinic is a 45-minute drop-in for whatever you’re actually stuck on.

No prior experience with AI is required. The focus is practical: what these tools can do, how to use them to save time on real work, and how to bring them into team projects and coursework.

Prefer to learn at your own pace? The Self-Paced Tracks turn these topics into self-guided pages with practice activities, readiness quizzes, and curated readings—36 topics across five tracks, from data analysis to governance and ethics.

ImportantUse AI to amplify your judgment, not replace it

The throughline across every workshop and track is the same: AI is a productivity tool, not a substitute for your expertise. Use it to get past the blank page, draft, summarize, and pressure-test your thinking—then bring your own judgment, taste, and accountability to the result. The goal is to develop the judgment for where AI needs close scrutiny (high-stakes, irreversible, or hard-to-verify work) and where it doesn’t—and to watch for cognitive offloading, handing over so much thinking that your own skills and voice quietly erode. More in Keeping Your Judgment: Cognitive Offloading & When to Scrutinize AI.

Core Sessions

Four one-hour sessions that everyone is welcome at, run every fall and repeated every spring. They build on each other, but each stands alone—come to all four, or drop into the ones most relevant to your work.

# Session What You’ll Do
Core 1 Foundations & Capabilities What generative AI is, which Drexel-approved tools to use, and prompting that gets useful results
Core 2 Workflow Enhancement Use ChatGPT Edu, Copilot Chat, and Microsoft 365 Copilot to speed up writing, email, summarizing, and Office tasks
Core 3 Reusable Assistants Turn prompts that work into shared Projects, custom GPTs, and simple agentic assistants your team or lab can reuse
Core 4 Verify, Disclose, Decide A hands-on hour on verification, funder and journal policy, disclosure, and the judgment calls in between

Slides and materials for each session are linked at the top of its page.

Electives

Offered once a year, by demand. Dates are announced by school email.

Elective What You’ll Do
Documents & Visuals with Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant for PDFs and Firefly for infographics and communication graphics
AI for Curriculum Development Design courses and build teaching materials with ChatGPT Edu, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Gemini Notebook
Data & Code for Research Staff (planned) AI for data cleaning, coding in Stata/R/Python, debugging, and reproducible analysis
AI for Grad Students (planned) Coursework, thesis and dissertation work, and the disclosure norms you’ll be held to

Until the two planned electives are scheduled, their material is already available self-paced: the Data & Analysis and Technical & Agentic tracks cover the research-staff topics, and the Learning Paths page has a graduate-student path.

Monthly AI Clinic

New this year. A 45-minute drop-in in the same slot each month. No registration, no agenda, no slides—bring real work: a prompt that isn’t behaving, a document you want to speed up, a disclosure question, a workflow you’d like to build. We work on it together. Come with a question or come to listen.

NoteA thread through every session: ethics & governance

Ethics and governance aren’t a bolt-on module—they run through the whole series. Cores 1–3 each open with a short beat tied to that day’s work: how AI fails and who’s accountable (Core 1), verifying and disclosing in daily workflows (Core 2), and data, consent, and oversight when assistants are shared (Core 3). Core 4 is where those beats come together into a full hands-on hour—verification you actually practice, what funders and journals now require, how to disclose, and how to decide what needs scrutiny. The electives apply the same thread to their own terrain (representation and bias in AI media; integrity, accessibility, and FERPA in teaching). Throughout, we work from Drexel policy and the ASPPH and WHO frameworks.

Who This Is For

The four core sessions are one shared foundation for the whole school. The activities are chosen to be useful whether you manage grants and schedules, coordinate research, teach and run a lab, or are working through an MPH, MS, or doctoral program.

  • Administrative staff — drafting, reformatting, summarizing, and the recurring documents that eat your week.
  • Research staff and coordinators — recaps, protocols, reports, and the data work that surrounds an analysis. For coding and analysis specifically, the Data & Analysis and Technical & Agentic tracks go deep, and a research-staff elective is planned.
  • Faculty — writing, literature, methods, teaching materials, and the disclosure expectations attached to each.
  • Graduate students — ChatGPT Edu became available to Drexel students in August 2026, and the norms around coursework, theses, and publication are shifting fast. The core sessions cover the tools and the rules; the Learning Paths page has a graduate-student sequence for coursework and for thesis/dissertation work. Your course AI policies still come from your instructors—these sessions help you read and follow them, not replace them.

Before You Come

  • Confirm your access to ChatGPT Edu (available to Drexel faculty, professional staff, and students — access details; see Tools & Access)
  • Confirm you can sign in to Microsoft Copilot Chat with your Drexel account (everyone), and to Microsoft 365 Copilot in Office if you hold that license (faculty and staff, extra cost)
  • Bring a laptop
  • Bring a real (non-sensitive) work task you’d like help with—an email to draft, a document to summarize, a report section or assignment to outline
  • Read the one-page data privacy rules below
WarningData Privacy — Read This First

Drexel-approved tools are not all equal on data:

  • ChatGPT Edu is licensed by Drexel and governed by Drexel’s ChatGPT Edu Terms of Use. Your prompts are not used to train OpenAI models. Use the current default model in the Drexel workspace unless a session tells you otherwise. Do not enter sensitive information that Drexel must protect by law, contract, or policy, including PHI.
  • Microsoft Copilot Chat with Enterprise Data Protection is listed by Drexel as usable with High Risk Data when signed in with a Drexel account, and Low Risk Data otherwise.
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams is listed by Drexel as usable with High Risk Data for licensed faculty and staff.
  • Zoom AI Companion is listed by Drexel as usable with High Risk Data when a meeting is not recorded or transcribed, and Low Risk Data when it is recorded or transcribed. Notify participants when it is active.
  • Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant, PDF Spaces, Creative Cloud, and Express are listed by Drexel as usable with Moderate Risk Data.
  • Research study data is stricter than the tiers: none of it goes into commercial/consumer AI tools (PII and HIPAA data absolutely never), and even de-identified, aggregate, or public study data needs the PI’s approval and must respect the IRB protocol, any data use agreement, and the data source’s terms of use. See the Research Data callout on Tools & Access.
  • Claude, ChatGPT personal, Gemini, Perplexity, and other consumer tools are Low Risk Data only unless separately approved by Drexel. Use these only with public or fully synthetic information.

When in doubt, de-identify, use synthetic data, or ask before entering anything sensitive. Drexel’s AI Tools page lists the approved data classification level for each tool. We cover this in detail in Core 1.
