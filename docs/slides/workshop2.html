WHICH TOOL TO OPEN

Two Tools, Same Steps

Copilot Chat

m365.cloud.microsoft/chat, signed in with your Drexel account. Good for quick summaries and rewrites of Drexel documents and email.

ChatGPT Edu

The Drexel workspace you used in Core 1. Good for longer drafting, going back and forth, and (in Core 3) saved assistants.

Same Steps in Both

Paste the text, or upload the file with the + button. Ask. Check. Copy the answer back into your document.

No Copilot inside Word or Excel at Drexel. You don't need it: upload the file or paste the text, then copy the answer back.

TOOLS & DATA

Same Data Rule as Core 1

Fine to use
  • Your own drafts, notes, and announcements
  • Committee and project email without sensitive details
  • Public documents and published papers
  • Made-up versions of anything sensitive
Never paste in
  • Health information about a person
  • Student records or Social Security numbers
  • Identifiable research data
  • When in doubt, leave it out or make the details up

JOB A · A DOCUMENT

Summarize and Rewrite a Document

Demo file · Workshop2_Demo_StudyDescription.docx

Pedestrian injuries remain a leading cause of traffic death in U.S. cities, and risk is concentrated at a small share of intersections. This study will link citywide crash records with street-level imagery to identify built-environment features — crossing distance, vehicle speeds, lighting, signal timing — associated with severe pedestrian injury at signalized intersections. Results will produce a ranked list of modifiable intersection features for city transportation partners…

Step 1 · SummarizeSummarize this document in 5 bullet points for a project status update.
Step 2 · Rewrite for an audienceRewrite the first paragraph for a community advisory board. No jargon, 8th-grade reading level, 4 sentences.
Step 3 · Draft new contentDraft a short “Next Steps” paragraph with three concrete steps.

Copy the rewrite into Word. Check it: does the plain version keep the facts right? Was anything added?

JOB B · AN EMAIL THREAD

Sort Out an Email Thread

Demo file · Workshop2_Demo_EmailThread.docx (6 messages, excerpt)

M. Rivera: …proposing we move EPI 570 to fall and add the data-visualization module. Thoughts before Friday?

J. Chen: Fall works if the lab sections don't collide with BST 555. Also — who owns the syllabus update?

M. Rivera: Agreed on fall, pending the registrar check. Syllabus update: can you take it, with a July 20 draft?

+ 3 more messages…

Step 1 · SummarizeSummarize this thread. List every decision made and every open question.
Step 2 · Structure itList the action items as a table: owner, task, due date.
Step 3 · Draft the replyDraft a reply confirming I'll have the syllabus draft ready by July 20. Friendly, two sentences.

Check it: did it list a decision nobody made? For your own email, try the Summarize button in Outlook.

ACTIVITY 2 · ~11 MIN

Your Own Task, Saved as a Recipe

  1. 1Pick a real task you need this week.
  2. 2Write it with PTCF: Persona, Task, Context, Format.
  3. 3Improve it twice: “Shorter.” “Put it in a table.” “What did you leave out?”
  4. 4Do the final edit yourself.
  5. 5Save the prompt in a “My prompts” document, with [blanks].
Example recipeYou are a [role]. Summarize [the document] for [audience] in [number] bullet points. Then list any names, dates, or numbers I should double-check.
No task? Use the abstracts fileSummarize each abstract as one row of a table: population, method, key finding. Then draft a 3-sentence email to my team on what these mean for our pedestrian safety work.

WRAP-UP

What to Take Away

Paste or upload, ask, check, copy the answer back. Copilot Chat or ChatGPT Edu both work.

  • Three moves cover most weekly tasks: summarize, rewrite for an audience, make a table.
  • Before you use it: Verified? Disclosed? Yours?
  • Save the prompts that work as recipes.
  • Before Core 3: save two recipes and use one again next week. Bring them.
